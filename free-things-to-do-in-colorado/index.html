Free Things To Do
In Colorado

One of America's great outdoor states is also one of its cheapest, if you know which four places to skip. Fifteen things that cost nothing — including one that is free by deed, forever.

What is free to do in Colorado?

Garden of the Gods, Red Rocks Amphitheatre outside show hours, the NCAR Mesa Lab exhibit floor, Dinosaur Ridge's public road, Bishop Castle, Bent's Old Fort, Paint Mines Interpretive Park, Denver Union Station, the elk rut in Estes Park, stargazing in the San Luis Valley, and hiking on any of Colorado's eleven national forests.

Here is the structural fact that makes Colorado cheap, and almost nobody explains it.

Eleven national forests cover an enormous share of Colorado's mountains, and hiking on national forest land does not cost anything. No gate, no pass, no reservation. Meanwhile, the fees you keep reading about are concentrated in a handful of famous places with parking problems.

Skip those four or five bottlenecks and most of this state has no admission at all.

The one that is free by law

Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs deserves its own paragraph. The land was donated to the city in 1909 on the explicit condition that it remain free to the public forever. No entrance fee, no parking fee, no reservation, permanently — because the deed says so.

Vertical red sandstone fins pushed upright by the same uplift that raised Pikes Peak, with paved accessible trails through the middle of them. It would be worth paying for. You do not have to.

The big one

Eleven National Forests,
No Gate

Colorado's national forests are the single largest free recreation resource in the state, and the least understood by visitors.

Hiking is free. Dispersed camping is free and legal on most Forest Service roads, generally capped at 14 days within a 28-day period. Scenic driving is free. There are no entrance stations on most of it.

What you give up is facilities. Dispersed sites have no toilets, no water and no bins, so you pack out everything — including waste. That is the trade, and it is a fair one.

Rules worth knowing before you go

  • Check the fire ban first. Restrictions are set county by county and by agency, and they change fast in a drought year. Colorado came out of the 2025–26 winter with the lowest snowpack in the modern record, so expect restrictions.
  • Camp on durable surfaces. Use the site that already exists, and stay at least 200 feet from lakes and streams.
  • Pack out everything. Orange peels and apple cores included — they take months to break down at altitude and teach animals to associate people with food.
  • Kill the fire properly. Drown it, stir it, drown it again, then hold the back of your hand above the ash. If you feel heat, it is not out.

Be honest about it

What Actually Does
Cost Money

Four bottlenecks account for most of the fees people complain about in Colorado. Knowing them lets you decide rather than be surprised.

  • Rocky Mountain National Park. Park entry plus a timed entry reservation from May 22 to mid-October 2026, with a $2 processing fee. Extra slots drop at 7 p.m. Mountain Time the night before.
  • Maroon Bells. $10 vehicle parking reservation, or $16 for the adult shuttle from Aspen Highlands. The road is shuttle-only 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Hanging Lake. $12 per person May through October, $10 November through April, permits available through December 2026.
  • Mount Blue Sky. Timed entry reservation and fee, now administered by Denver Mountain Parks after the road's 2026 reopening.

State parks also charge, either a daily vehicle pass or the annual Keep Colorado Wild Pass, which residents can add to a vehicle registration at a discount. That is worth understanding: state parks charge, national forests generally do not. It is the most useful distinction in Colorado recreation and the one visitors most often get wrong.

Everything else on this page remains free. So if the budget is tight, the honest advice is to build the trip around the national forests and the free landmarks, and treat the four bottlenecks as optional splurges rather than obligations.

Common questions

Free Colorado, Answered

What is free to do in Colorado?

Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs, Red Rocks Amphitheatre outside show hours, the NCAR Mesa Lab exhibit floor in Boulder, Dinosaur Ridge's public road in Morrison, Bishop Castle near Rye, Bent's Old Fort in La Junta, Paint Mines Interpretive Park in Calhan, Denver Union Station, the elk rut in Estes Park, stargazing anywhere in the San Luis Valley, and hiking on any of Colorado's eleven national forests.

Is Garden of the Gods free?

Yes, permanently. The land was donated to the City of Colorado Springs in 1909 on the explicit condition that it remain free to the public forever. There is no entrance fee, no parking fee and no reservation requirement. It is one of the few major American attractions with free access written into its deed.

Can you visit Red Rocks for free?

Yes. Red Rocks Amphitheatre is free to walk on any day nothing is scheduled, along with the surrounding trail system and the visitor centre. Locals run the amphitheatre stairs at sunrise. You only pay when there is a ticketed event, and the geology alone justifies the visit.

Is hiking free in Colorado?

On national forest land, yes. Colorado has eleven national forests covering a large share of the mountains, and hiking there costs nothing. Fees are concentrated in a handful of famous places with parking problems — Rocky Mountain National Park, the Maroon Bells, Hanging Lake and Mount Blue Sky. Skip those four bottlenecks and most of Colorado has no gate at all.

What can you do in Denver for free?

Walk Red Rocks in Morrison, see the Cretaceous dinosaur footprints along the public road at Dinosaur Ridge, sit in the great hall at Union Station, walk the RiNo mural district, and visit the free exhibit floor at the NCAR Mesa Lab in Boulder — an I.M. Pei building with free parking and trails leaving from the lot.

Are Colorado state parks free?

No. Colorado state parks charge a daily vehicle pass or require an annual Keep Colorado Wild Pass, which residents can add to a vehicle registration at a discount. National forest land, by contrast, is generally free to hike and to camp on in dispersed sites. That distinction is the single most useful thing to understand about free recreation here.

Is camping free in Colorado?

Dispersed camping on national forest land is free and legal on most Forest Service roads, generally capped at 14 days within a 28-day period. There are no facilities, so you pack out everything including waste. Developed campgrounds with toilets and water charge a fee and book through Recreation.gov, often months in advance.

What is the best free thing to do in Colorado?

Garden of the Gods is the strongest single answer — vertical red sandstone fins, paved accessible trails, and free forever by deed since 1909. For something stranger, Bishop Castle near Rye is a hand-built stone castle with a fire-breathing metal dragon and no admission charge. For scale, stargazing in the San Luis Valley under some of the darkest skies in the lower 48 costs nothing at all.
