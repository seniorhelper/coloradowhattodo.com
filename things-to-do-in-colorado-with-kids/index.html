The Altitude Problem
Nobody Warns You About

How does altitude affect kids in Colorado?

Children are affected faster than adults, and it usually shows up as irritability, poor sleep and loss of appetite rather than anything dramatic. Sleep your first night at a lower elevation, keep everyone drinking more water than feels necessary, and save the most demanding activity for day three. If a headache with nausea will not resolve, the treatment is to descend.

Here is the arithmetic. The percentage of oxygen in the air never changes — it is about 20.9% everywhere. What changes is air pressure, and pressure is what pushes oxygen into blood.

At Denver's 5,280 feet, air delivers roughly 82% of the oxygen it does at sea level. At 10,000 feet, about 69%. That is not a small difference, and small bodies notice it sooner.

Families routinely land, drive straight to a mountain town at 9,000 feet, and spend day two wondering why everybody is miserable. Then they blame the trip.

Four things that fix it

  • Sleep low on night one. Denver or Colorado Springs before Breckenridge or Estes Park. One night makes a measurable difference.
  • Water, constantly. Dry air pulls moisture out through the lungs at a rate nobody notices. Dehydration looks exactly like altitude sickness.
  • Easy day first. A museum, a flat lake walk, a town. Save the big hike for day three.
  • Sunscreen, seriously. Ultraviolet radiation increases roughly 4 to 5 percent per 1,000 feet. Children burn here on cool cloudy days.

None of this is a reason to avoid Colorado with children. It is a reason to plan the first 48 hours differently than you would anywhere else. Our full guide to reading a Colorado mountain day covers the physiology in more detail, including a working oxygen calculator.

By age

Different Ages,
Different Answers

Toddlers and preschool

Keep it low and keep it short. Denver Union Station's great hall is free, indoors and endlessly interesting to small children — trains arrive, people move, and nobody minds noise. Red Rocks is free to walk and the scale alone does the work. The Denver Museum of Nature & Science has a dedicated discovery area.

Skip the fourteeners, obviously, and skip anything requiring a two-hour drive each way.

Primary school age

Primary school age is when Colorado clicks. At Dinosaur Ridge in Morrison, kids stand beside real Cretaceous footprints on a tilted rock slab, in the town that gave the Morrison Formation its name. Sliding down the Great Sand Dunes is tiring and totally new. The Durango & Silverton steam train follows an 1882 narrow-gauge line through a river canyon, a long day that kids remember.

The elk rut in Estes Park in September and October is free, spectacular and viewed from the car. Hundreds of elk, bugling at dawn and dusk. Keep your distance — a bull elk in rut is a 700-pound animal with a bad attitude.

Teenagers

Raise the intensity. Rafting the Arkansas through Browns Canyon, the Royal Gorge Bridge 956 feet above the river, mountain biking, or Meow Wolf Convergence Station, which is four floors of immersive art with a story hidden inside it.

Fourteeners become plausible around this age, provided everyone is acclimatised and you start before sunrise. Mount Elbert at 14,440 feet is the highest in the state and one of the more walkable.

Plan B

When The Weather Turns

Afternoon storms are near-daily from mid-July into early September. A washed-out afternoon is normal here, so have the indoor list ready before you need it.

  • Denver Museum of Nature & Science. Dinosaurs, gems and a planetarium, plus mountain views from the west windows. Easily half a day.
  • Meow Wolf Convergence Station. Four floors, three hours minimum, and better for older children than young ones.
  • NCAR Mesa Lab, Boulder. Weather and climate exhibits inside an I.M. Pei building, with no admission, no parking charge and trails starting right outside. One of the best-value hours a family can spend.
  • Hot springs. The huge Glenwood pool is right by I-70, and once everyone is in the water, rain stops mattering.
  • Denver Union Station. Warm, free to wander, full of food, and the airport train pulls in here in about 37 minutes.

The other constant is the two o'clock rule. Colorado's summer storms build on a daily cycle you can plan around: mornings are reliably clear, afternoons are not. Do the outdoor thing first and hold the museum for later.

Camping With Kids

Families on a budget can camp for free on most Forest Service roads in Colorado's national forests. Colorado Camping Spots lays out where to go by region, and which sites have water and which very much do not.

Common questions

Colorado With Kids, Answered

What are the best things to do in Colorado with kids?

The family favorites are sledding the Great Sand Dunes, a day at the Denver Museum of Nature & Science, the Durango & Silverton steam train, Dinosaur Ridge's footprints just outside Denver, fall elk watching in Estes Park and the Royal Gorge Bridge, 956 feet above the Arkansas River.

How does altitude affect kids in Colorado?

Children are affected faster than adults, and it usually shows up as irritability, poor sleep and loss of appetite rather than anything dramatic. Sleep your first night at a lower elevation such as Denver or Colorado Springs before going higher, keep everyone drinking more water than feels necessary, and save the most demanding activity for day three. If a headache with nausea will not resolve, the treatment is to descend.

Is Colorado good for family vacations?

Very. Four national parks, a steam railroad, dinosaur fossils you can stand next to, dunes you can sled down and a free museum floor on atmospheric science all sit within a few hours of Denver. The main planning constraints are altitude on day one and driving distances — Denver to Durango is about six and a half hours, so pick one region rather than three.

What can kids do in Denver?

The Denver Museum of Nature & Science in City Park has dinosaurs, gems and the Gates Planetarium. Meow Wolf Convergence Station is four floors of immersive art that works well for older children. Union Station's great hall is free to sit in, and Dinosaur Ridge in Morrison — with real Cretaceous footprints on a tilted rock slab — is about twenty minutes away and free to walk.

Is Great Sand Dunes good for kids?

For many families it is the highlight of the trip. Rent a sled or board in Alamosa and ride North America's tallest dunes, about 750 feet high, until the kids are exhausted. The one real hazard is heat: on summer afternoons the sand can pass 150°F, so plan mornings or evenings.

What is there to do in Colorado with kids in winter?

Ski school is the obvious answer, and most Colorado resorts have one. Snowshoeing needs no lesson and costs roughly what a lunch at the lodge does, and hot springs round out the day. Glenwood Hot Springs, one of the biggest geothermal pools anywhere, sits right off I-70, which makes it the dependable plan when the weather changes.

Are Colorado national parks free for kids?

Most national park fees are per vehicle, so kids usually ride along at no extra cost, and the National Park Service offers a free annual pass to fourth graders and their families. Rocky Mountain National Park also needs a timed entry booking from May 22 through mid-October 2026, with a $2 processing fee added to the entry charge.

How long should a family spend in Colorado?

Give it about a week in a single region. That leaves room for a low-elevation day to adjust, a national park visit and some unscheduled time. Squeezing the Front Range, the mountains and the Western Slope into less than a week means the kids spend their vacation strapped into a car seat.
