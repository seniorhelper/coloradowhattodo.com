What is there to do in Colorado?

Colorado has four national parks — Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde, Great Sand Dunes and Black Canyon of the Gunnison — plus 58 peaks above 14,000 feet, 26 designated scenic byways, natural hot springs, skiing from December through March, and 13 International Dark Sky Parks. Denver adds immersive art, major museums and dinosaur footprints twenty minutes from downtown.

There is no shortage of Colorado lists. What there is a shortage of is lists that tell you the thing that actually ruins trips: half the best places now need a reservation you were supposed to book weeks ago.

So this page does two jobs. First, a filterable index of thirty-six places worth your time. Second, a plain table of what currently needs a permit, what it costs, and when the window closes — verified for the 2026 season rather than copied from an older guide.

Colorado is also bigger than people expect. Denver to Durango is roughly six and a half hours. Denver to the Great Sand Dunes is four. Pick a region and go deep rather than trying to touch three.

The part other guides get wrong

The 2026 Reservation
Reality Check

Do you need reservations for things to do in Colorado?

For several of the most popular places, yes. Rocky Mountain National Park, the Maroon Bells, Hanging Lake and Mount Blue Sky all require advance reservations during their operating seasons, and all of them regularly sell out. Turning up without one is the most common way visitors get turned around at the gate.

This is the section that goes stale fastest, so here is what we verified for the 2026 season as of August.

Rocky Mountain National Park

Timed entry runs May 22 to October 13 for most of the park, and May 22 to October 18 for the Bear Lake Road corridor. Two permit types, two-hour entry windows, no set departure time. The processing fee is $2 on top of park entry. Extra slots drop at 7 p.m. Mountain Time the night before each arrival date — that is the one almost nobody knows.

Maroon Bells

Vehicle access runs May 15 to October 31 with a $10 parking reservation. The RFTA shuttle from Aspen Highlands runs May 22 to October 18 at $16 for adults. The road is shuttle-only between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m., so drive before 8 or after 5. E-bikes now pay a $5 amenity fee because the Forest Service classifies them as motorised.

Hanging Lake

Permits are available through December 2026 — $12 per person May through October, $10 November through April. The trail is 1.2 miles each way with about 1,200 feet of gain. The trailhead at I-70 Exit 125 is eastbound access only, so coming from Denver you pass it, take Exit 119 and double back. The trail can close on short notice after heavy rain because of the burn scar, and permits are refunded when it does.

Mount Blue Sky

Reopened over Memorial Day weekend 2026 after nearly two years of construction. Timed entry is required, and Denver Mountain Parks now runs reservations and collects the fee — the Forest Service used to staff the entrance. The road climbs more than 7,000 feet in 28 miles and is the highest paved road in North America.

Three rules that cover everything else

  • Book official, not resellers. Recreation.gov and the agency or chamber sites are the real portals. Third-party sites charging inflated prices are reselling something they do not control.
  • The last two weeks of September are the hardest dates of the year. Peak aspen colour plus good weather plus school-free travellers. Harder to book than July 4th.
  • Arrive before the window or after it. Most of these systems only restrict the middle of the day. Dawn and evening are frequently unrestricted, quieter and better lit.

There Are Five Colorados,
Not One

What are the different regions of Colorado?

Colorado divides into five practical travel regions: the Front Range urban corridor along I-25, the High Country of the central Rockies, the Western Slope beyond the Continental Divide, the San Luis Valley in the south-central basin, and the Eastern Plains. They differ by hours of driving, thousands of feet of elevation and entire ecosystems.

Front Range

Denver, Boulder, Colorado Springs, Fort Collins. Roughly four-fifths of the population, in about a tenth of the area. Museums, red rock, breweries, and foothills trailheads thirty minutes from downtown.

High Country

Summit County, Vail, Aspen, Leadville, Estes Park. The ski resorts, the fourteeners and Rocky Mountain National Park. Highest average elevation and the shortest summer.

Western Slope

Grand Junction, Durango, Telluride, Ouray, Mesa Verde. Desert canyon country meeting the San Juans. Warmer, drier, emptier, and the best fall colour in the state.

San Luis Valley

Alamosa, Great Sand Dunes, Crestone. A high desert basin at roughly 7,500 feet, ringed by fourteeners, with some of the darkest night skies in the lower 48.

Eastern Plains

La Junta, Sterling, Lamar. Almost nobody goes. Which is exactly why the dinosaur tracks, the adobe forts and the clay hoodoos out here are still yours alone.

The mistake first-time visitors make is trying to touch three of these in five days. Colorado is roughly the same size as the entire United Kingdom, and it has mountains in the middle of it. Pick the region that matches your season: High Country in July and September, Western Slope in May, June and October, Front Range and Eastern Plains any time, San Luis Valley in late spring or autumn.

Common questions

Colorado, Answered Plainly

What is there to do in Colorado?

Colorado has four national parks — Rocky Mountain, Mesa Verde, Great Sand Dunes and Black Canyon of the Gunnison — plus 58 peaks above 14,000 feet, 26 designated scenic byways, natural hot springs, world-class skiing from December through March, and 13 International Dark Sky Parks. Denver adds immersive art, major museums and dinosaur footprints twenty minutes from downtown.

What is the number one thing to do in Colorado?

Rocky Mountain National Park is the most visited single attraction, drawing millions of people a year to 355 miles of trail, roughly 150 lakes and Trail Ridge Road, the highest continuous paved highway in North America at 12,183 feet. It requires a timed entry reservation from May 22 through mid-October 2026.

Do you need reservations for things to do in Colorado?

For several of the most popular places, yes. Rocky Mountain National Park, the Maroon Bells, Hanging Lake and Mount Blue Sky all require advance reservations during their operating seasons and regularly sell out. Book through the official agency or chamber portals rather than third-party resellers. The last two weeks of September, when the aspens peak, are the hardest dates of the year.

What is free to do in Colorado?

Garden of the Gods, Red Rocks Amphitheatre outside show hours, the NCAR Mesa Lab exhibit floor, Dinosaur Ridge's public road, Bishop Castle, Bent's Old Fort, Paint Mines Interpretive Park, Denver Union Station, stargazing anywhere in the San Luis Valley, and hiking on any of Colorado's eleven national forests. Garden of the Gods was donated to Colorado Springs in 1909 on the condition it stay free forever.

How many days do you need in Colorado?

Five to seven days for one region, done properly. Colorado is roughly the size of the United Kingdom with mountains in the middle — Denver to Durango is about six and a half hours of driving each way. Trying to combine the Front Range, the High Country and the Western Slope in under a week means spending the trip in the car.

Is Mount Blue Sky open in 2026?

Yes. The Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway reopened over Memorial Day weekend 2026 after nearly two years of construction on the upper road. Timed entry reservations are required, and reservations and fees are now administered by Denver Mountain Parks rather than the U.S. Forest Service. It is the highest paved road in North America.

What is Colorado best known for?

The Rocky Mountains, skiing, and its 58 fourteeners. Colorado also has the highest mean elevation of any U.S. state at roughly 6,800 feet, and is the only state entirely above 3,300 feet — its lowest point, on the Arikaree River, is higher than the highest point in twenty other states.

When is the best time to visit Colorado?

September is the best single month — stable weather, thinner crowds after Labor Day, the elk rut and peak aspen colour. June through August opens everything but brings near-daily afternoon thunderstorms and the most reservation requirements. December through March is ski season. April, May and early November are cheapest, though many high roads stay closed.
