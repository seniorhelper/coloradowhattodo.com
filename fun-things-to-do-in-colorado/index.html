Not the polite list. Sandboarding 750-foot dunes, a castle one man has been building since 1969, the highest paved road on the continent, and the darkest skies in the lower 48.

What is fun to do in Colorado?

For a first trip, aim for the experiences that feel unlike anywhere else: riding a board down 750-foot dunes in the San Luis Valley, a warm soak at Glenwood or Strawberry Park, a guided raft trip through Browns Canyon on the Arkansas, the drive up Mount Blue Sky (North America's highest paved road) and a night under one of the state's 13 certified International Dark Sky Parks.

"Fun" is a different question from "impressive." Plenty of Colorado is impressive. Less of it is the thing you will still be talking about on the flight home.

So this page filters for the second thing. Everything here either gets your heart rate up, gets you somewhere you cannot get anywhere else, or is simply so odd that it becomes the story.

The three that always win

If You Only Do Three

1. Sandboard the Great Sand Dunes

Picture a 750-foot wall of sand leaning on the 13,000-foot Sangre de Cristos, with nobody telling you where to go. Pick up a board or sled in Alamosa, slog to the top and let gravity handle the rest.

It is harder than it looks. Climbing loose sand at 8,000 feet is genuinely tiring, and there is no shade anywhere. But it is the single most unusual thing you can do in this state, and it works equally well for adults and children.

Take the heat seriously: on summer afternoons the sand can top 150°F. Mornings and evenings are both cooler and better light, and shoes are not optional.

2. Drive the highest paved road in North America

From Idaho Springs, Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway gains over 7,000 feet in 28 miles and finishes just under a 14,000-foot summit. Up there, seeing mountain goats and bighorn sheep is the expectation, not a stroke of luck.

After close to two years of work on the upper road, it opened again for Memorial Day weekend 2026. You need a timed reservation, and Denver Mountain Parks, not the Forest Service, now runs the booking and collects the fee. Plenty of older guides still say otherwise.

The road is narrow, has minimal guardrail, and vehicles over 30 feet are not permitted. It is not technically difficult. It is psychologically memorable.

3. Get in the water

There are two ways to do it. The noisy way is a raft on the Arkansas, America's most-rafted river, where trips range from easy family floats to Class IV rapids in Browns Canyon.

The calm way is a hot spring. Glenwood's giant pool sits right beside I-70, and Strawberry Park near Steamboat is a set of stone terraces in a creek canyon. Soaking in 104-degree water while snow lands in your hair may be the single best part of a Colorado winter.

In a low-snow year like 2026, the big-water rafting window compresses. Ask the outfitter what the gauge is reading rather than what the brochure says.

The genuinely weird

Colorado's Strangest Days Out

Every state has scenery. Colorado has a surprising amount of eccentricity, and almost all of it is free.

Bishop Castle, near Rye

Jim Bishop started stacking stone in 1969 and, more or less, kept going for the rest of his life. The result has towers, iron walkways and a metal dragon that breathes fire. Nobody charges you to climb it, and nothing about the railings meets a modern safety code. It is pure obstinacy turned into architecture, and it is a blast.

Picketwire Canyonlands, near La Junta

Out on the eastern plains, a remote canyon floor holds North America's biggest set of dinosaur tracks, hundreds of them. Getting there is long, hot and dry, which is precisely why you will probably have it to yourself. Save it for spring or fall.

Paint Mines Interpretive Park, Calhan

Striped clay spires and hoodoos that could pass for a corner of southern Utah, set down in the grassland east of Colorado Springs. No fee, a short drive from the city, and reliably quiet.

The state is not a rectangle

Not somewhere to go, but the best fact to bring. Colorado's boundary has 697 sides rather than four, because nineteenth-century surveyors with compasses and chains made hundreds of small errors and the Supreme Court ruled in 1925 that the mistakes were the legal border. The Four Corners Monument sits roughly 1,800 feet from the mathematically correct point, and courts have consistently ruled the marker wins.

Timing

Fun By Season

Spring, April to May

High passes are still gated and mountain trails are mud. However, the Western Slope is at its best — Colorado National Monument, Mesa Verde and the desert canyons are perfect before summer heat. Rafting season begins as runoff picks up, and mountain-town lodging bottoms out between ski season and summer.

Summer, June to August

Everything is open and everyone is here. Trail Ridge Road, the high passes, the alpine lakes, the fourteeners. Afternoon thunderstorms build almost daily from mid-July, so summit early and be below treeline by two.

Autumn, September to October

If you can only pick one month, pick September. The weather settles, crowds drop once school is back, elk are bugling around Estes Park and the aspens start turning by mid-month. Color follows elevation, not the calendar: the high passes go gold first and the foothills last.

Winter, November to March

Skiing, obviously. But the underrated version is hot springs, snowshoeing and empty desert. Shelf Road near Cañon City has rock climbing in January. Colorado National Monument under snow is close to deserted.

The constraint is driving. Westbound I-70 on a Friday and eastbound on a Sunday are genuinely brutal during ski season, and CDOT activates traction and chain restrictions during storms. Check road conditions or dial 511 before you leave.

Common questions

Fun In Colorado, Answered

What is fun to do in Colorado?

For a first trip, aim for the experiences that feel unlike anywhere else: riding a board down 750-foot dunes in the San Luis Valley, a warm soak at Glenwood or Strawberry Park, a guided raft trip through Browns Canyon on the Arkansas, the drive up Mount Blue Sky (North America's highest paved road) and a night under one of the state's 13 certified International Dark Sky Parks.

What is the most fun thing to do in Colorado?

Ask around and sandboarding at Great Sand Dunes usually wins. Grab a board or sled in Alamosa, hike up North America's tallest dunes and ride back down. It is hard work, nothing else in Colorado feels like it, and adults enjoy it as much as kids. Avoid the midday heat in summer.

Is Colorado fun in the summer?

Yes, and it is the season when almost everything is open — Trail Ridge Road, the high passes, the rivers and the fourteeners. The catch is afternoon thunderstorms, which become near-daily from mid-July into early September. The rule is to be off any summit by noon and below treeline by two. Mornings are reliably clear.

What is there to do in Colorado besides skiing?

A great deal. Rafting, hot springs, sandboarding, scenic driving on 26 designated byways, stargazing, mountain biking, hot air ballooning, and four national parks. Denver alone has immersive art at Meow Wolf, dinosaur footprints at Dinosaur Ridge twenty minutes away, and a museum holding roughly 95% of one major painter's entire life output.

Are there hot springs in Colorado?

Plenty. Glenwood Hot Springs, beside I-70, is among the biggest geothermal pools in the world. Strawberry Park near Steamboat Springs offers stone-terraced natural pools in a creek canyon, though in winter the access road often calls for four-wheel drive or a shuttle. Ouray and Pagosa Springs also have major developed springs.

What is the weirdest thing to do in Colorado?

Start with Bishop Castle near Rye, a stone fortress one man began by hand in 1969 and kept adding to, complete with towers, iron walkways and a fire-breathing metal dragon; entry is free and the railings are not what you would call modern. Then there is Picketwire Canyonlands, home to North America's largest dinosaur trackway, and the Paint Mines near Calhan, whose striped hoodoos look lifted from Utah.

Can you drive to the top of a mountain in Colorado?

Yes. Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway, North America's highest paved road, gains more than 7,000 feet over 28 miles. It opened again for Memorial Day weekend 2026 after almost two years of construction, and a timed reservation, handled by Denver Mountain Parks, is required. Pikes Peak is another option, with both a highway and a cog railway to the top.

Where can you see the stars in Colorado?

With 13 International Dark Sky Parks, eight Dark Sky Communities and more than 30 further sites working toward certification, Colorado leads the world in the program. Around Great Sand Dunes, the San Luis Valley has some of the darkest skies in the lower 48. Time your trip for a new moon and let your eyes adjust for half an hour.
