What is fun to do in Colorado?

Sandboarding the 750-foot dunes at Great Sand Dunes, soaking in natural hot springs at Glenwood or Strawberry Park, rafting the Arkansas River through Browns Canyon, driving Mount Blue Sky — the highest paved road in North America — and stargazing in one of Colorado's 13 International Dark Sky Parks. Bishop Castle near Rye is free and genuinely strange.

"Fun" is a different question from "impressive." Plenty of Colorado is impressive. Less of it is the thing you will still be talking about on the flight home.

So this page filters for the second thing. Everything here either gets your heart rate up, gets you somewhere you cannot get anywhere else, or is simply so odd that it becomes the story.

The three that always win

If You Only Do Three

1. Sandboard the Great Sand Dunes

The tallest dunes in North America, roughly 750 feet, piled improbably against the 13,000-foot Sangre de Cristo range. You rent a board or a sled in Alamosa, climb, and ride down.

It is harder than it looks. Climbing loose sand at 8,000 feet is genuinely tiring, and there is no shade anywhere. But it is the single most unusual thing you can do in this state, and it works equally well for adults and children.

One warning worth taking seriously: the sand surface can exceed 150°F on summer afternoons. Go in the morning or the evening, and wear shoes.

2. Drive the highest paved road in North America

Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway climbs more than 7,000 feet in 28 miles, ending just below a 14,000-foot summit. Mountain goats and bighorn sheep are routine sightings rather than lucky ones.

It reopened over Memorial Day weekend 2026 after nearly two years of construction on the upper section. Timed entry reservations are required, and they are now administered by Denver Mountain Parks rather than the Forest Service — a change most guides have not caught up with.

The road is narrow, has minimal guardrail, and vehicles over 30 feet are not permitted. It is not technically difficult. It is psychologically memorable.

3. Get in the water

Two versions. The loud one is rafting the Arkansas River, the most-rafted river in the country, which runs everything from gentle family floats to Class IV whitewater in Browns Canyon.

The quiet one is hot springs. Glenwood is enormous and right off I-70. Strawberry Park outside Steamboat is stone-terraced pools in a creek canyon, and sitting in 104-degree mineral water while it snows on your head is arguably the best thing about Colorado winter.

In a low-snow year like 2026, the big-water rafting window compresses. Ask the outfitter what the gauge is reading rather than what the brochure says.

The genuinely weird

Colorado's Strangest Days Out

Every state has scenery. Colorado has a surprising amount of eccentricity, and almost all of it is free.

Bishop Castle, near Rye

A full stone castle with towers, iron catwalks and a fire-breathing metal dragon, built almost single-handedly by Jim Bishop starting in 1969. He largely never stopped. There is no admission fee and there are no safety railings in the modern sense. It is a monument to sheer stubbornness and it has to be seen in person.

Picketwire Canyonlands, near La Junta

The largest dinosaur tracksite in North America — hundreds of prints across a remote canyon floor on the eastern plains. The approach is long, hot and waterless, which is exactly why almost nobody is ever there. Not a summer trip.

Paint Mines Interpretive Park, Calhan

Banded clay hoodoos and spires that look like southern Utah was dropped onto the Colorado prairie. Free, close to Colorado Springs, and consistently empty.

The state is not a rectangle

Not somewhere to go, but the best fact to bring. Colorado's boundary has 697 sides rather than four, because nineteenth-century surveyors with compasses and chains made hundreds of small errors and the Supreme Court ruled in 1925 that the mistakes were the legal border. The Four Corners Monument sits roughly 1,800 feet from the mathematically correct point, and courts have consistently ruled the marker wins.

Timing

Fun By Season

Spring, April to May

High passes are still gated and mountain trails are mud. However, the Western Slope is at its best — Colorado National Monument, Mesa Verde and the desert canyons are perfect before summer heat. Rafting season begins as runoff picks up, and mountain-town lodging bottoms out between ski season and summer.

Summer, June to August

Everything is open and everyone is here. Trail Ridge Road, the high passes, the alpine lakes, the fourteeners. Afternoon thunderstorms build almost daily from mid-July, so summit early and be below treeline by two.

Autumn, September to October

The best month in Colorado is September and it is not close. Stable weather, thinner crowds after Labor Day, the elk rut in Estes Park, and aspens turning from mid-month. Colour runs by elevation rather than by date — high passes first, Front Range foothills last.

Winter, November to March

Skiing, obviously. But the underrated version is hot springs, snowshoeing and empty desert. Shelf Road near Cañon City has rock climbing in January. Colorado National Monument under snow is close to deserted.

The constraint is driving. Westbound I-70 on a Friday and eastbound on a Sunday are genuinely brutal during ski season, and CDOT activates traction and chain restrictions during storms. Check road conditions or dial 511 before you leave.

Common questions

Fun In Colorado, Answered

What is fun to do in Colorado?

Sandboarding the 750-foot dunes at Great Sand Dunes, soaking in natural hot springs at Glenwood or Strawberry Park, rafting the Arkansas River through Browns Canyon, driving Mount Blue Sky — the highest paved road in North America — and stargazing in one of Colorado's 13 International Dark Sky Parks. Bishop Castle near Rye, a full stone castle built almost single-handedly since 1969, is free and genuinely strange.

What is the most fun thing to do in Colorado?

For most people it is sandboarding at Great Sand Dunes National Park. You rent a board or sled in Alamosa, climb the tallest dunes in North America, and ride down. It is physically demanding, completely unlike anything else in the state, and it works for adults and children equally. Go early or late — the sand surface can exceed 150°F on summer afternoons.

Is Colorado fun in the summer?

Yes, and it is the season when almost everything is open — Trail Ridge Road, the high passes, the rivers and the fourteeners. The catch is afternoon thunderstorms, which become near-daily from mid-July into early September. The rule is to be off any summit by noon and below treeline by two. Mornings are reliably clear.

What is there to do in Colorado besides skiing?

A great deal. Rafting, hot springs, sandboarding, scenic driving on 26 designated byways, stargazing, mountain biking, hot air ballooning, and four national parks. Denver alone has immersive art at Meow Wolf, dinosaur footprints at Dinosaur Ridge twenty minutes away, and a museum holding roughly 95% of one major painter's entire life output.

Are there hot springs in Colorado?

Many. Glenwood Hot Springs off I-70 is one of the largest geothermal pools anywhere. Strawberry Park outside Steamboat Springs is stone-terraced natural pools in a creek canyon — though the winter road frequently needs four-wheel drive or a shuttle. Ouray and Pagosa Springs both have significant developed springs as well.

What is the weirdest thing to do in Colorado?

Bishop Castle near Rye. Jim Bishop began building a stone castle by hand in 1969 and largely never stopped. It has towers, iron catwalks and a fire-breathing metal dragon. There is no admission fee and there are no safety railings in the modern sense. Picketwire Canyonlands, which holds the largest dinosaur tracksite in North America, is a close second.

Can you drive to the top of a mountain in Colorado?

Yes. Mount Blue Sky Scenic Byway is the highest paved road in North America, climbing more than 7,000 feet in 28 miles. It reopened over Memorial Day weekend 2026 after nearly two years of construction, and now requires a timed entry reservation administered by Denver Mountain Parks. Pikes Peak also has a highway and a cog railway to the summit.

Where can you see the stars in Colorado?

Colorado has 13 International Dark Sky Parks and eight Dark Sky Communities, with more than 30 places statewide in the certification process — more than anywhere else on Earth. The San Luis Valley, around Great Sand Dunes, has some of the darkest skies in the lower 48. Go near a new moon and give your eyes 30 minutes to adjust.
